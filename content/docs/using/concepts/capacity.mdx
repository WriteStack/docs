title: Capacity
description: How many Notes you can have scheduled at once on each WriteStack plan, which Notes count toward the limit, and what happens when you reach it.
---

Your plan sets how many Notes can be scheduled at the same time. A slot frees up each time a scheduled Note publishes, so you can keep queueing as Notes go out.

## Limits per plan

| Plan | Notes scheduled at a time |
| --- | --- |
| Hobbyist | 20 |
| Standard | 100 |
| Enterprise | Unlimited |

The **Unlimited Schedules** add-on removes the limit on any plan. It is a one-time payment. Prices and how to buy it are on [Plans and limits](/docs/account/plans-and-limits).

## What counts

A Note counts toward the limit when all of these are true:

- its status is scheduled,
- its posting time is in the future,
- it is [synced](/docs/using/concepts/sync) to Substack.

These don't count: drafts, Ready Notes, published Notes, and scheduled Notes that are not synced to Substack yet. Moving a Note that is already scheduled to another time doesn't use a new slot.

## When you reach the limit

| Where | What you see | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scheduling a Note in WriteStack | A dialog titled **Schedule Limit Reached**: "You have reached your limit of N active schedules." | Wait for a scheduled Note to publish, remove one from the queue, upgrade, or buy **Unlimited Schedules**. |
| Syncing Notes that were scheduled elsewhere (for example by an AI assistant) | A dialog titled **You've reached your scheduling limit**. It shows how many Notes fit and how many don't. | Click the button to sync the ones that fit and move the rest to Ready. Click **Not now** to leave them as they are. |

When you sync past the limit, WriteStack syncs the earliest Notes first. The rest move to **Ready**. Nothing is deleted. Schedule them again once space frees up.

## For AI assistants

An assistant connected through MCP can read your remaining capacity with `get_schedule_capacity` and should never schedule more than that. See [Build with WriteStack](/docs/build).
